Effective warm ups and cool downs

According to physiotherapy experts, a poor warm up routine is a leading cause of many injuries such as knee and ankle injuries.

Dynamic vs Static Stretches

Before the Game: Dynamic Stretching

  • What: Controlled, repetitive movements (no holding).
  • Why: Increases blood flow, wakes up the brain-body connection, and boosts performance. Research shows static stretching before a game actually decreases your speed and power.

After the Game: Static Stretching

  • What: Holding positions for 20 to 60 seconds.
  • Why: Best done while muscles are already warm to safely improve long-term flexibility and lower your heart rate.

Court and Pivot

(Netball, Basketball and Volleyball) - Preventing Knee & Ankle Injuries

High risk movements like sharp cutting and landing cause the majority of youth ACL tears, sprains, and Jumper's Knee.

How to Protect Your Joints:

  1. Land Softly: Landing with bent knees instead of straight legs slashes ligament impact force by up to 60%.
  2. Activate Stabilizers: Use dynamic landing drills, hops, and ankle circles during your warm-up to wake up the muscles that prevent ankles from rolling.

Field and Contact

(Rugby, Football, Touch) - Hamstrings, Ankles, & Concussion Awareness

High speed sprinting and unpredictable playing surfaces put youth athletes at risk for sudden injuries.

  • The Power of Balance: Research shows that proprioceptive training (balance and body awareness) can reduce the risk of ankle sprains by 28% to 81%.
  • Protecting the Hamstrings: Strengthening the hamstring muscles specifically is the most effective way to prevent painful tears during full-speed sprints.
  • Smart Warm Up Additions: Incorporate single-leg balance drills to stabilize ankles for uneven grass, alongside focused hamstring activation exercises.

Stick and Agility

(Cricket and Hockey) - Spine, Wrist, & Shin Care for Stick Sports

The repetitive loading, fast bowling, and hard turf in hockey and cricket frequently cause lower back pain, hand impact injuries, and shin splints.

  • Protecting the Lower Back: Research shows that building mid-back (thoracic) mobility and core stability is the best way to shield the lower back from heavy rotational stress.
  • Prepare for Impact: Grip and ball contact place high demands on the upper body. Dynamic joint prep prevents wrist and forearm strain.
  • Smart Warm Up Additions: Include thoracic rotations to let the spine move freely, alongside dynamic wrist and forearm circles to brace joints for high-impact ball contact.
