Most Common Types of Injuries and How They Occur

The primary focus for each of these groups will be:

  • Court and Pivot - ACL/Knee, Ankle sprains, Jumpers Knee and injuries that occur from fast changes and movements.
  • Field and Contact - Hamstring tears, Concussion awareness and Ankle issues
  • Stick and Agility - Lower back, wrist/hand impact, shin splints, and injuries that occur from fast changes and movements.

Court and Pivot

(Netball, Basketball and Volleyball) - Preventing Knee & Ankle Injuries

ACL/Knee, Ankle sprains, Jumper's Knee, and injuries that occur from fast changes and movements.

When sports require sudden shifts in direction and high momentum, your joints take the hit. This is what to look out for:

  • ACL Tear: Occurs when you plant your foot to change direction but your body keeps moving forward, twisting a bent knee.
  • Ankle Sprain: Happens during cuts or landings. Tired stabilizing muscles fail to react in time, leading to painful ligament tears.
  • Jumper's Knee: An overuse injury of the patellar tendon. Continuous jumping without adequate rest creates micro-tears that can't heal.

Field and Contact

(Rugby, Football, Touch) - Hamstrings, Ankles, & Concussion Awareness

Fast paces combined with unpredictable ground create a perfect storm for contact and field athletes.

Here is what to watch for:

  • Hamstring Tear: Happens during max sprinting when the muscle tries to brake for landing, forcing it to stretch and contract at the same time.
  • Concussion: Triggered by whiplash, tackles, or impacts that cause the brain to shift rapidly, stretching cells and causing a chemical imbalance.
  • Issues: Caused by heavy running loads on uneven surfaces, like hidden divots or stepping on an opponent's boot, forcing the joint out of alignment.

Stick and Agility

(Cricket and Hockey) - Spine, Wrist, & Shin Care for Stick Sports

In sports requiring repetitive, one sided movements, like bowling or hockey your body often operates under uneven pressure.

Here are the most common injury risks:

  • Lower Back Pain: Often caused by a persistent crouched posture that compresses the spinal disks. Furthermore, the "wringing effect" from powerful, one sided rotational movements puts significant stress on the spine, leading to muscle strains and potential stress fractures.
  • Shin Splints: A common overuse injury resulting from the high-impact, repetitive force of running or pivoting on hard surfaces, which inflames the tissue along the shin bone.
  • Wrist Injuries: These are typically acute, occurring when the wrist is subjected to sudden, high intensity force that the small carpal bones cannot fully absorb.
