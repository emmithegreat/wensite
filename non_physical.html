Non Physical Information

Non Physical Information page covers the other important things youth athletes should focus on Off the court/pitch to prevent injuries. This includes Nutrition, Hydration and Sleep.

Importance of Nutrition in Sports Injury Prevention

Proper nutrition insures that an athlete has optimal performance, muscle strength and endurance, Bone health and immune function.

Keep these three nutrition pillars in mind:

  • Stay Energized: Good nutrition fights off fatigue, preventing the tired mistakes that lead to sudden joint or ligament injuries.
  • Repair with Protein: Prioritize protein to maintain muscle strength and protect yourself from painful strains and tears.
  • Strengthen Bones & Immunity: Get enough calcium, vitamin D, and magnesium to prevent stress fractures, while a balanced diet keeps your immune system strong enough to stay on the field.

The key nutrients in injury prevention are Protein, Omega 3, Vitamin C, Calcium and Vitamin D.

When/What should you eat:

1-3 Hours before game: Digesting carbohydrates such as Oatmeal with fruit, white or brown rice with lean chicken, or whole-wheat pasta with a light tomato sauce.

30-60 Minutes after: High protein and carbohydrate rich meals to help with muscle repair, such as meals that contain poultry, meats, fish, or legumes.

What to avoid: High fat and fibre food.

Importance of Hydration

Hydration is an important factor of injury prevention and plays a crucial role to prevent illness, and sure that an athlete is playing at its best ability.

It is important for athletes to stay hydrated because performance depends on it, and also because lots of fluids are lost from activity. Research shows that "People can lose up to 2 quarters of fluid" for every hour of activity.

There are many other reasons why hydration is important when playing sports:

  1. Hydration is important to ensure that athletes can fulfill their maximum speed and flexibility. Hydration also cushions the joints, which is important for preventing injuries.
  2. Hydration is also needed to make sure that the muscles work properly and also avoid cramps, which again - prevents injury.
  3. It is also easier to breathe when being hydrated because it is easier to absorb oxygen through your lungs.

Water VS Electrolytes: Water is primarily best, however electrolytes is best for physical activities that are highly demanding for over an hour, hot conditions and high amounts of sweat.

Other information:
  • Athletes aged 13-18 should have 3 Litres of water a day
  • Athletes aged 9-12 drink 1.5 to 2 Litres of water a day
  • Look at Urine colour, if its yellow drink more
  • Avoid energy drinks as source of hydration

Importance of Sleep

Sleep is a crucial part of injury prevention, with those who sleep less than 8 hours each night are 1.7 times more likely to get injured than those who sleep 8 or more hours.

How Much Sleep Should I get:

  • 8-10 Hours a night
  • 12 and Under should get 9 - 12 hours of sleep

From a school survey, 28% of student athletes are not getting enough sleep, therefore are at a higher risk of getting injured.

Why Sleep is important:

Without good sleep, research shows that players get fatigued when playing sports quicker, which doesn't directly impact young athletes chance of getting injured, however if players fatigue quicker it means that they are then at more risk of getting injured.

Sleep is also needed for muscle recovery, repair and growth.
